## LinkedIn

In rural India, 3 out of 4 Class 3 children can't yet read a Class 2 story (ASER 2024). The fix
is known: listen to each child read, catch the mistake, practise it, again and again. One
teacher with 40 children can't do that every day.

So we built Akshara, a reading tutor that listens, in Kannada, Tamil and Hindi.

A child reads a story aloud. Gnani's Prisma hears every word. The words they missed are broken
into aksharas, and Gnani's Timbre says each part until the child can read the whole word. Evon,
Gnani's open language model, is wired in to write practice sentences for the exact sounds that
child finds hard, with a teacher approving each one; its first batch is still to run. The teacher sees the whole class and listens only to the
marks worth doubting.

We didn't just build it, we measured it: 90 passages, three languages, four audio conditions, the
results published inside the app on a "How reliable is this?" page. It works offline, deletes
recordings after 30 days and adds no child without a parent's consent.

Tested on synthetic voices so far; children are next.

Built by Achindra (the scorer, the Gnani integrations, the reliability study) and Daniel (the
web app, and the Kannada and Tamil text).
